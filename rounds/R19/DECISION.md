## Reproducible synthetic micro-test, executed in JS
Three fictional groups with 20 children each, each must complete the same 30-minute activity in one of four pre-approved slots. Only one group can occupy that protected place per slot, ordinary other programme assumed protected and equivalent, staffing/meal/safety feasibility represented by allowed slots (not modeled independently). Entries are fictional round-trip unprotected minutes per child; null means unavailable/forbidden. These are NOT measured heat doses, approved local safety thresholds, actual groups or site data.

| A | 12 | 6 | 7 | -- |
| B | 10 | -- | 16 | 8 |
| C | -- | 3 | 12 | 13 |
Exhaustive enumeration of 11 feasible complete assignments:
| approach | A/B/C slots (1-indexed) | child-minutes exposed |
| earliest feasible sequential | 1/3/2 | 620 |
| individually cheapest sequential | 2/4/3 | 520 |

All three complete activities; no extra capacity or lost service modeled. Versus individually cheapest sequential reduction 160 child-minutes/day, 30.77%, or 2.667 child-hours/day. Versus earliest 260 (41.94%). This is constructive counterexample to greedy scheduling, not comparison to skilled operator or proof real scheduling is suboptimal. Skilled manual global plan can equal 360; no incremental value then.
