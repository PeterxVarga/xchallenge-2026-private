## 1. Decision: a performance-based two-skin outdoor classroom
The concrete architectural idea for the HŐTÁBOR competition is a reusable, open-side, low-radiant-heat activity space: an ordinary shade canopy first, a compatible upgrade with a separate ventilated low-emissivity lower membrane OR sky-facing high solar-reflectance radiative-cooling material, and movable activity furniture using existing approved stock. Not one compulsory proprietary material, not a compulsory new pavilion, not an improvised retrofit. The primary M1 service bottleneck is still lunch/program/supervision across sites; this structure cannot solve it by itself. Its distinct goal is additional usable outdoor teaching/childcare capacity during summer and shoulder seasons, only where actual 2027 stock and planned shelters leave a gap. Extreme heat -> approved indoor alternative. No public after-hours access, no mandated winter use, no expanded district target or overnight accommodation. A protected room (M2) remains separate.
ROI alternatives: (1) new bespoke high-tech PDRC pavilion: visible but price, local performance, structural approval and existing tents overlap unproven; (2) conventional white canopy/optimized existing spaces: best if same benefit and full service; (3) modular performance-based architecture with comparable single white, low-e double membrane and PDRC variants (selected at concept level): means lower technology dependence and a more defensible upgrade; (4) powered fans/evaporative/actively chilled system: can help under specific conditions but water/electrical, hygiene, noise and maintenance increase complexity. Do not make a false compromise by combining all technology. **New contest physical lead is the ventilated double skin / low-e underside**, because closely related European field evidence directly compares it against a conventional white membrane. PDRC is a credible alternative, not mandatory. This is a research-led preference, not a demonstrated winner for Kőbánya. None of these hardware branches is approved; A/no additional spending remains first if sufficient.

## 3. Concrete concept, not construction drawing
One illustrative 30–36m² nominal canopy over a flexible furniture/activity zone, perpendicular open sides unobstructed by unauthorized skirts/walls; actual geometry/occupancy, openings, material spec, frame and allowable uses to be established by qualified designers. R84 30m² /3m example had 58.5% worst-case target-area shading after grid optimization; 48m² example 83.5%, NOT fully protected area. Prefer actual safe use-time/target-zone placement and sufficient area rather than a forced rectangular size. Double skin uses separated layers so ventilation and freely drained geometry remain possible; independent whole-assembly structural suitability is mandatory. Do not suspend a loose liner from the existing tarp, block vents, modify tension system or assume absence of anchorage requirements. PDRC branch needs unobstructed sky view and manufacturer-approved whole assembly; if used as upper layer, lower layer properties and air gap require renewed thermal, optical and structural verification, not addition of separate research benefits. No suggestion to buy or install now.
Modularity is functional: (i) occupied space and open-air ventilation; (ii) interchangeable by qualified installer only, compatible thermal skin options; (iii) furniture layout for outdoor lessons, craft, quiet activity and supervised breaks; (iv) standardized seasonal maintenance and approved F indoor fallback. Shade arrangement does not automatically permit eating, weatherproof storage or year-round structural exposure. Meals require R83 service approvals; no forced change to lunch site just for physical visibility. Spring/autumn use may increase actual hours; optional summer skin removal/storage under approved design, not winter heating claim. Existing 4–5 proposed ordinary tents are baseline. Heritage location R84 imposes extra controls. Fire behavior of full assembly, wind/snow/rain loads, drainage, anchorage/stability, accessibility, clearance, egress, safe handling, vandalism, lighting/glare, cleaning, UV degradation and replacement documented by qualified parties. EN13782 series may be relevant depending on structure/scope, actual Hungarian adopted edition and other rules to be checked; no simplified exemption or legal approval. ## 5. Physical screening with two independent mechanisms
Transmitted shortwave through two ideal plane layers, neglect diffuse and angle effects but allow incoherent multiple reflections: tau_eff = tau1*tau2/(1-rho1_back*rho2_front). If the extracted material values were valid in the matching directions (.10,.06,.76,.68), tau_eff=1.24%, compared with single10%; 700W/m² incident -> 70 versus 8.69 W/m² transmitted. Illustrative 30m² horizontal projection -> 2.10 versus 0.261 kW, difference 1.839 kW. This is NOT net cooling, MRT or room energy, actual textile angle, reflectance of both specific surfaces, absorption, diffuse radiation, seams and side entry not known. No claim specific material property used in correct direction without appropriate datasheet.
Longwave-only counterexample to assuming higher emissivity always better: grey diffuse underside exchanging with35°C effective background, roof view fraction0.25, T_eq^4=T_bg^4+f*epsilon*(T_roof^4−T_bg^4) in Kelvin. Reflected longwave approximation treats incident background uniform; not actual full MRT/PET, person/clothing or heat budget. Temperature and emissivity are independent sensitivities; no coupled thermal solution.

If underside warmer than background, low-e can reduce radiant exposure; if cooler, high-e can be more helpful in this model. Therefore neither double skin nor PDRC wins universally. Full evaluation requires upper/lower spectral reflectance, directional transmittance and emissivities, actual geometry, air-gap ventilation, roof/ground surface, humidity/clouds, air velocity, load and season. Conventional WHITE high-performance reference must be used; not dark/asphalt-only.
