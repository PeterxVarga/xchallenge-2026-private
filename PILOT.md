## Hypothesis and what is new locally
The recovered archive of the 2026 Tekergő camp report (current reopen failed) gives concrete evidence of disrupted programming in severe heat and burdensome transfers to cooler premises. The existing 2027 proposal for AC and 4–5 shelters is the comparison condition, not the project's innovation. Test one intervention: targeted additional usable shaded activity capacity at a specifically verified remaining hot zone, with a repeatable shade placement and operation protocol. Allocate new modular shade only for the marginal gap after existing assets are optimised; ordinary shade is the control technology. Do not claim all children can stay outside safely, or that shading replaces air-conditioned refuges. If funded baseline eliminates gap, KILL this project and evaluate housing/institutional reserve.

## Impact measure
Primary: incremental safe, usable shaded activity child-hours compared with planned baseline on suitable days, aggregated, not unique children or health outcomes. Illustrative lower 20 children × 1 hour × 30 days = 600 child-hours per season; upper 60 × 1.5 × 30 = 2700. Three seasons assumed unchanged: 1800/8100 hours; cost per incremental child-hour ~1728/384 HUF at modeled TCO. Capacity, attendance, weather, usage, and authorized activity must be measured. Do not use the 100–140 number as daily unique impact; 2026 report describes the number in shade context, not a measured daily average. No invented avoided health costs. Secondary: paired empty-zone WBGT, radiant exposure, useful shade m²-hours, reprogrammed sessions and staff workflow. Stop operations under relevant school safety/heat/wind conditions. No deliberate exposure of children to unshaded hot controls.
